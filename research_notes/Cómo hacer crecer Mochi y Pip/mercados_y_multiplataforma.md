# International markets, multi-language strategy and multi-platform distribution for "Mochi & Pip" (non-verbal kids' cartoon Shorts), 2025–2026

Research date: 2026-11-23. Research method note: the session's network blocked direct fetches of support.google.com, blog.youtube, techcrunch.com and cartoonbrew.com, so several YouTube/TikTok facts below come from search-result summaries of those pages or from secondary coverage. Each point is flagged where this matters. Anything that could not be sourced sits under "Gaps".

## 1. Where the audience is: countries by volume vs. ad value (RPM/CPM) for kids content

### Takeaway
By sheer audience, YouTube's biggest markets are India (~490–520M), then the US (~250M+), Indonesia and Brazil (~140–150M each). These volume markets pay roughly 10–20x less per view than the US, UK, Australia or Switzerland. "Made for Kids" (MFK) cuts RPM again on top of that: kids/teens is the lowest-paying niche, at roughly $0.3 RPM on average. So for a non-verbal MFK Shorts channel, volume markets (India, Indonesia, Brazil, Mexico, the Philippines, Vietnam, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Egypt) supply the views, and the small share of US/UK/DE/AU/Nordic views supplies most of the money.

### Cited Findings
- **India** is YouTube's largest market, with about 491M users (ad reach, Jan/Feb 2025). DataReportal ranks it first in its "top ten territories" by YouTube ad reach. — [DataReportal, Essential YouTube Stats 2025](https://datareportal.com/essential-youtube-stats)
- **Brazil** has about 144M and **Indonesia** about 143M YouTube users (ad reach, Jan 2025). — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/essential-youtube-stats)
- DataReportal itself warns that its ad-reach figures are "not a direct proxy" for a platform's total user base. — [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/essential-youtube-stats)
- Secondary aggregators give higher, later numbers: India 500–518M, US 259M, Indonesia 151M, Brazil 150M. YouTube reports 2.58B active users as of Oct 2025. These are aggregators citing DataReportal/YouTube, not primary sources. — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/youtube-users); [SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/youtube-statistics-2/)
- **Shorts-specific:** Google's India Brandcast figure is **650M+ monthly Shorts viewers in India**. The source is an aggregator table quoting Google. Another aggregator's figures for Brazil (97.4M Shorts MAU, 9% of global Shorts views) and Indonesia (6.8% of Shorts users) are internally inconsistent. Treat them as unreliable. — [TechRT Shorts statistics](https://techrt.com/youtube-shorts-statistics/); [Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/youtube-shorts-statistics-engagement-metrics)
- **CPM by country (all content, 2025):** US about $14.67, Australia $13.30, Switzerland $12.98, Pakistan $0.53. One analysis puts US viewers at about $14.67 per 1,000 ad impressions versus about $0.74 for India, a roughly 20x gap on identical content. — [MilX](https://milx.app/cases/in-what-countries-cpm-are-the-highest); [Digital Information World, Aug 2025](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html) (DIW gives a US *median* of $11.95. US figures conflict across datasets: $8.50–$15.)
- **RPM by country (music channels, 2025–26):** the average across all markets is $1.09 RPM. Australia is highest at $6.80. — [Dynamoi, YouTube RPM by country 2025](https://dynamoi.com/learn/statistics/youtube-rpm-by-country-2025); [Dynamoi data 2026](https://dynamoi.com/data/youtube-adsense-rpm.html)
- **The kids penalty:** a 300-channel study (AIR Media-Tech, May 2025–May 2026) puts Kids & Teens RPM at **$0.33**, versus $10.22 for Education & Science. In the same study, kids channels showed an ad on only **32% of views**, versus 77% for education. These figures came through search summaries of the CPM/RPM articles listed above. The exact article hosting the study was not verified. — search set incl. [awisee CPM study](https://awisee.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rates), [MilX](https://milx.app/ru/cases/why-you-should-track-revenue-per-minute-not-just-per-view)
- Kids channels typically see a **CPM of $1–$4**, versus $8–$20+ for comparable adult content. The cause is that MFK content cannot serve personalized ads under COPPA. — [vidIQ, YouTube CPM](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-cpm/)
- **Older context (2024):** in the UK, 48% of 5–7-year-olds had their own profile on YouTube or YouTube Kids, up from 39%. — [Ofcom 2024 release](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/news-centre/2024/a-window-into-young-childrens-online-worlds)
- **UK 2025:** 96% of 8–14s use YouTube and 58% use TikTok (Ofcom passive measurement 2025). Social media use among 3–5s rose from about 3 in 10 (2023) to almost 4 in 10 (2025). Parents mostly run or supervise the accounts: 36% use the platforms on the child's behalf and 42% supervise. — [Ofcom passive online measurement 2025 (PDF)](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/ofcom-childrens-passive-online-measurement-2025.pdf); [Twinkl summary of Ofcom 2025](https://www.twinkl.com/news/more-3-5-year-olds-are-using-social-media-and-consuming-online-content); [Ofcom Children & Parents 2025 report](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-children/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025)

### Inferences
- A non-verbal cartoon has no language barrier, so the algorithm will naturally push it into the largest and cheapest pools first: India, Indonesia, Brazil, Pakistan, Bangladesh, the Philippines and Egypt. Expect most views from there and an effective RPM well below $0.10 per 1,000 Shorts views. This is an inference from the $0.33 kids RPM, which is mostly long-form, and Shorts' generally lower RPM.
- With Shorts revenue pooled and allocated by view share, a view from the US/UK/DE/AU/Nordics is plausibly worth 10–20x a view from India or Pakistan. It is better to measure "revenue per day" by geography in YouTube Analytics than to chase raw views.
- Practical trade-off: volume markets build subscribers and the scale that unlocks YPP. High-RPM markets drive income. The two are not in conflict for a dialogue-free format. The levers that tilt toward high-RPM geographies are upload time (US morning/afternoon), English titles first, and Western-style thumbnails and humour.
- MFK Shorts also lose comments and notifications. That makes thumbnails and titles in the viewer's language (which parents see) more important.

### Gaps
- I found no official country breakdown of **Shorts** views, and no RPM-by-country dataset **specific to Made-for-Kids** content. Country RPMs above are all-content or music datasets.
- No reliable 2025–2026 numbers for kids-animation viewing in Mexico, the Philippines, Vietnam, Pakistan, Bangladesh or Egypt. DataReportal country reports would be the next stop, but that was not fetched.
- No primary data on Shorts RPM for MFK specifically.

## 2. YouTube multi-language features in 2025–2026 (titles, audio tracks, auto-dubbing) and which languages to add

### Takeaway
YouTube now has three layers. (1) Translated titles and descriptions have long existed and work on Shorts. (2) Multi-language audio tracks: creators upload their own dubbed tracks. This opened to all creators in Sept 2025 and works for "a single video or Short". (3) AI auto-dubbing: it expanded to YPP creators in 2025 and to everyone in Feb 2026, covering 27 languages, with Shorts dubbed automatically on upload. For a cartoon **with no dialogue**, audio tracks and auto-dubbing add little or nothing. Translated titles and descriptions, plus on-screen text kept to a minimum, are the relevant lever.

### Cited Findings
- **Multi-language audio**, all creators, **September 2025**: the feature expanded to all creators, ending a roughly two-year pilot. Listed languages include English, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese and Spanish. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/brands-that-skip-translation-risk-losing-global-audiences); [TechCrunch, 10 Sept 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/); [Business Today, 11 Sept 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/youtube-launches-multi-language-audio-for-millions-of-creators-extending-global-reach-with-auto-dubbing-493458-2025-09-11)
- YouTube says creators who uploaded multi-language audio tracks in the pilot saw **over 25% of their watch time come from views in the video's non-primary language**. This is a pilot average, it measures watch time, and there is no Shorts-specific number. — [YouTube Blog, multi-language audio](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/) (via search summary; direct fetch blocked)
- The YouTube Help page "Add multi-language features to your videos" covers translated titles, descriptions and audio tracks, and says they can be applied to "a single video or Short". The feature does **not** generate dubs. Creators must upload their own audio tracks. — [YouTube Help 13338784](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en) (via search summary; direct fetch blocked)
- **Auto-dubbing timeline:** launched with selected creators in 2024 in 8 languages. It expanded to YPP creators in 2025, and then "all new content, both Shorts and VODs, will be automatically dubbed upon upload". On **5 Feb 2026** it became available to everyone with **27 languages**. It added expressive speech, lip-sync (described in some coverage as still in development), viewer "Preferred Language" settings, and creator opt-out or own-upload options. — [YouTube Blog, auto-dubbing expressive speech](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-auto-dubbing-expressive-speech/); [India TV News, 5 Feb 2026](https://www.indiatvnews.com/technology/news/youtube-auto-dubbing-feature-expands-to-all-users-now-supports-27-languages-2026-02-05-1029070); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/media/youtube-expands-ai-auto-dubbing-to-27-languages-with-expressive-speech-1.500433271); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-auto-dubbing-to-all-creators/811375/); [Google India blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/unlocking-a-global-audience-with-auto-dubbing/)
- Sources conflict on intermediate language counts. One says 9 languages plus 11 more, which totals 20. The 27-language figure comes from Feb 2026 coverage. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-auto-dubbing-to-all-creators/811375/) vs. [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/media/youtube-expands-ai-auto-dubbing-to-27-languages-with-expressive-speech-1.500433271)
- A 2026 analysis of YouTube says brands that skip translation risk losing global audiences. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/brands-that-skip-translation-risk-losing-global-audiences)

### Inferences
- **Audio tracks and auto-dubbing:** both are built for speech. Auto-dubbing has nothing to translate in a dialogue-free cartoon. Uploading an "Indonesian audio track" identical to the original adds no information. The channel should leave auto-dubbing on, since it is harmless, or switch it off. It should not spend effort on audio tracks. The exception would be adding a short sung or narrated intro or jingle, which is not recommended because it breaks the universal format.
- **Translated titles and descriptions** are the only multi-language metadata lever that applies. YouTube shows the translated title to users whose interface language matches. That plausibly lifts click-through in feeds and search, especially where parents search in their own language. On the Shorts swipe feed, where the title is barely visible, the effect is probably small. I found **no published, measured uplift** from translated titles alone. The 25% figure applies to audio tracks.
- **Priority languages to add, inferred from audience size, low saturation and RPM:**
  1. **Arabic**: Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE and the Gulf. Gulf RPMs are mid-to-high, and Egypt has volume.
  2. **German**: a high-RPM market, and listed in the multi-language rollout.
  3. **Japanese**: high RPM, and a strong cat-character culture.
  4. **Vietnamese** and **Filipino/Tagalog**: high-volume Shorts markets. Note that many Filipino parents use English UIs, so Filipino is lower priority.
  5. **Turkish** and **Russian**: large YouTube populations. Russia has had YouTube throttling and monetization constraints since 2022, which is low value.
  6. **Korean** and **Thai**: mid priority.
  7. **Urdu and Bengali** (Pakistan, Bangladesh) could also be considered for volume.
- Title translation costs almost nothing, so adding 10–15 languages is cheap. The real constraint is title quality: use native-reviewed, short titles.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch YouTube Help pages directly to confirm whether auto-dubbing skips videos with no speech, or how it treats MFK content.
- No study or YouTube statement quantifies reach uplift from translated **titles** alone (as opposed to audio tracks).
- The full official list of the 27 auto-dub languages was not retrieved.

## 3. TikTok: kids content rules, reach for cartoons, Creator Rewards, India ban

### Takeaway
TikTok's global minimum age is 13. Users under 13 in the US are routed into a curated, view-only "Under 13 Experience" whose content is chosen by Common Sense Networks, so creators cannot "target" that feed directly. A kids' cartoon can still reach the 13+ and adult audience on TikTok, since cute cat-and-mouse humour is all-ages. TikTok monetization via the Creator Rewards Program requires videos **longer than 1 minute**, 10K followers and 100K views in 30 days, and is available only in selected countries. Mochi & Pip's 23–45 s format therefore does not qualify. TikTok remains **banned in India**, confirmed by the government in Aug 2025, which removes the biggest volume market.

### Cited Findings
- **Under-13 experience (US):** "In the US, we offer a curated, view-only experience for those under age 13… We partner with Common Sense Networks to select content that it deems to be both age-appropriate and safe for an audience under 13." Commenting, messaging and sharing are removed. — [TikTok Transparency, Protecting teens](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/protecting-teens/); [TikTok Newsroom, 8 Oct 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/an-update-on-our-work-to-provide-teens-with-age-appropriate-experiences)
- From **13 Sept 2025**, new US users identified as under 13 are automatically placed in that restricted experience. — [theAsianparent PH, TikTok youth safety updates 2025](https://ph.theasianparent.com/tiktok-youth-safety-updates-2025)
- In January 2026 TikTok updated how it enforces age-appropriate experiences. — [TikTok Newsroom, 16 Jan 2026](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/updates-to-how-we-enforce-age-appropriate-experiences?lang=en-GB)
- Enforcement is weak. A May 2026 Maldita.es investigation found TikTok left over 97% of flagged self-declared under-13 accounts online, and 62.5% of those accounts were created in 2025. — [Maldita.es, 20 May 2026](https://maldita.es/policy/20260520/tiktok-minors-under-13-reports/)
- Academic analysis of the US "Kids Mode / Under 13 Experience" is in [arXiv 2507.00299 (UC Irvine, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.00299v1).
- **Creator Rewards Program:** the minimum age is 18 (19 in South Korea), with 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days. Videos must be original and **longer than one minute**. Duets, stitches, Photo Mode and sponsored posts are excluded. Business accounts are excluded. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creator-fund/); [Brandwatch](https://www.brandwatch.com/blog/tiktok-creator-program/)
- Eligible countries conflict across 2025–2026 guides. The commonly cited core is US, UK, Germany, France, Japan, South Korea, Brazil and Mexico, with some guides adding Italy and Canada. **Spain, India and Indonesia are not on these lists.** The authoritative list is only shown in-app. — [Quasa, eligible countries 2026](https://quasa.io/media/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-eligible-countries-in-2026); [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/how-to-join-the-tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- **India ban:** imposed in June 2020 under Section 69A of the IT Act. On 23 Aug 2025 the government called reports of it being lifted "false and misleading". Brief website access was traced to a network misconfiguration, and TikTok said it had not restored access. — [Business Today, 23 Aug 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/no-comeback-for-tiktok-indian-govt-refutes-reports-of-ban-being-lifted-on-chinese-apps-490769-2025-08-23); [All India Radio / NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/no-order-issued-to-lift-ban-on-tiktok-in-india-govt)
- In the UK, 58% of 8–14s use TikTok despite the 13+ age rule (Ofcom 2025). — [Ofcom passive measurement 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/ofcom-childrens-passive-online-measurement-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- On TikTok, Mochi & Pip should be positioned as **all-ages / family humour** ("cozy cat comedy"), not as "for kids". Explicitly kid-targeted framing risks restricted distribution, and the platform's core users are 13+. Do not use hashtags like #forkids or #kidsvideos aimed at minors.
- For monetization, TikTok would need **compilations longer than 60 s**, for example 2–3 episodes stitched as "Mochi & Pip – 3 stories". The creator must also be in an eligible country. A Spain-based creator would not qualify under the cited lists, though Mexico would. TikTok is therefore mostly a **reach and brand-building** channel, not a revenue channel, for this format.
- With India excluded, TikTok's large volume markets for this content are Indonesia (TikTok's largest or second-largest market), Brazil, Mexico, the US, the Philippines and Vietnam. Pakistan has had intermittent bans.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how well children's cartoons perform on TikTok's For You feed versus other niches.
- I did not retrieve TikTok's exact current Community Guidelines text on "content for younger audiences", or the official in-app CRP country list.

## 4. Instagram Reels & Facebook Reels: reach, monetization, cross-posting/originality, children's content

### Takeaway
Meta rewards **original** content and demotes reposts. Since 2024, identical reposted Reels are swapped for the original in recommendations. In April 2026 this became **account-level**: accounts that are mostly unoriginal are no longer recommended to non-followers. Facebook pays creators through **Content Monetization**, which unified Reels, photos, stories and text and is invite-only with an interest form. Since March 2026 it is also reachable through **Creator Fast Track**: three months of guaranteed pay for creators with 20K+ followers elsewhere. Meta's monetization policies are stricter than its general rules. Third-party guides say children's content is often restricted from ad monetization, but I could not confirm this from Meta's own page.

### Cited Findings
- **Instagram originality (2024):** "the original content will directly replace the reposted content in the app's recommendations". Reposts carry a credit label, and significantly changed content is not replaced. — [TechCrunch, 30 Apr 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/04/30/instagram-is-updating-its-ranking-systems-to-surface-more-content-from-smaller-original-creators); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- **Expansion (Apr 2026):** the originality policy now covers photos and carousels as well as Reels, and enforcement is **account-wide**. If most of an account's posts are unoriginal, it is not recommended to non-followers. Collab and credited-repost tools stay viable. Mosseri: "look for ways to make content your own so you can continue to be recommended to people who don't follow you." — [PetaPixel, 30 Apr 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/); [Planoly](https://planoly.com/blog/instagram-updates-its-original-content-policy)
- **Facebook Content Monetization** pays for "all eligible formats… reels, photos, stories and text". It is **invite-only**, and creators can join an interest list via Professional Dashboard → Monetization → Content Monetization. Facebook had said it planned open enrollment in 2025, but I found no confirmation that this happened. — [Facebook Creators, Content Monetization](https://creators.facebook.com/tools/facebook-content-monetization?); [Facebook Creators blog](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/new-tips-to-help-you-earn-on-facebook-content-monetization); [Fortune, 14 Mar 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/03/14/facebook-creators-monetization/)
- In June 2025 Facebook said **all videos will be shared as Reels**, removing the separate video and Reels distinction. — [Facebook Creators blog, "making it easier to create videos"](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/making-it-easier-to-create-videos-on-facebook)
- **Creator Fast Track (18 Mar 2026):** three months of guaranteed pay for sharing eligible Reels, for creators with **20,000+ followers** on another platform. Accepted creators get immediate access to Content Monetization. — [Meta Newsroom, 18 Mar 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/creator-fast-track-grow-your-audience-earn-money-on-facebook/); [TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/world/2026-03-18-facebook-unveils-three-month-creator-program-to-take-on-youtube-tiktok/)
- Facebook monetization requires compliance with the Partner Monetization Policies, Community Standards and Content Monetization Policies. Content acceptable on Facebook "isn't necessarily appropriate for monetization". — [Facebook Creators, guidelines](https://creators.facebook.com/guidelines); [Fluxnote, FB Reels requirements 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/facebook-reels-monetization-requirements-2026)
- One third-party guide says children's content has "stricter policies; often restricted or not eligible for ad monetization due to compliance with laws like COPPA". This concerns Ad Breaks and is unverified against Meta's own pages. — [Ranktracker](https://www.ranktracker.com/blog/ad-break-requirements-for-specific-content-types-on-facebook)
- Older guidance (2017): Facebook's monetization guidelines exclude videos that misappropriate children's media characters. This is not relevant to original IP. — [Tubefilter, 2017](https://www.tubefilter.com/2017/09/13/facebook-ad-monetization-guidelines/amp/)

### Inferences
- Mochi & Pip is original IP, so it **benefits** from the originality push, provided the same account is the original poster everywhere. Do not let fan or aggregator accounts post first. Upload to Meta natively, with no TikTok or CapCut watermark.
- Facebook's older-skewing audience (parents, grandparents) plus pet and cat humour is a plausible fit for **family-humour framing**. The channel should not label itself "content for children", given unverified indications that kids-directed content faces monetization limits and that Meta's platforms are 13+.
- The 20K-follower Creator Fast Track threshold is reachable using YouTube subscribers as the "other platform" audience. Once YouTube passes 20K subs, apply.

### Gaps
- I could not find Meta's current primary text on whether child-directed content is ineligible for Content Monetization. Its Content Monetization Policies page needs to be read directly.
- No data on actual Facebook or Instagram Reels RPM for animation.
- No reach benchmarks for kids cartoons on Reels.

## 5. YouTube Kids app, kid-safe platforms (Kidoodle.TV etc.), FAST and licensing

### Takeaway
Marking content MFK does **not** automatically place it in YouTube Kids. Inclusion is governed by YouTube's quality principles for children's content, which also affect MFK monetization. Kid-safe AVOD/FAST outlets such as Kidoodle.TV, Common Sense's Sensical and pocket.watch channels acquire **curated libraries** through deals, not self-serve uploads. They become relevant once Mochi & Pip has a library of episodes, compilations and proof of audience.

### Cited Findings
- Videos set as made for kids "are not automatically included in the YouTube Kids app". — [YouTube Help, MFK FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541)
- YouTube's quality principles for kids and family content guide decisions "both for inclusion in YouTube Kids and channel and video monetisation". — [YouTube Help, Best practices for children's and family content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223?hl=en-GB)
- MFK disables features including channel memberships, notifications, posts and comments. — [YouTube Help, MFK FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541); [YouTube Help, watching MFK content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en)
- Creators choose MFK or not per Short at upload. — [YouTube Help, Get started creating Shorts](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)
- vidIQ says MFK channels' Shorts are eligible for Shorts revenue sharing. This is unverified against YouTube's own documentation. — [vidIQ, Made for Kids 2026](https://www.vidiq.com/blog/post/make-money-kids-youtube-channel)
- **Possible future YPP change, reported and unverified:** Cartoon Brew reports that from **1 Feb 2027**, new channels applying for full monetization will need **20M qualified Shorts views in 90 days**. The current path is 10M Shorts views in 90 days, with 1,000 subscribers. The article's headline says it "will push animators away from Shorts". I could not fetch the article to confirm details. — [Cartoon Brew](https://cartoonbrew.com/shorts/youtube-shorts-monetization-rule-changes-265682.html)
- In 2021 YouTube warned it would demonetize low-quality kids content. — [TechCrunch, 25 Oct 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/25/youtube-warns-creators-it-will-start-to-demonetize-low-quality-kids-videos-next-month) (older)
- **Kidoodle.TV** is a kid-safe AVOD/FAST service owned by A Parent Media Co. It picked up 850 Mattel episodes, with FAST and AVOD rights, in July 2024. It added TheSoul Publishing's family content, and in 2021 launched an analytics portal for content creators. — [Kidscreen, 25 Jul 2024](https://kidscreen.com/2024/07/25/kidoodle-tv-picks-up-850-episodes-from-mattel); [PR Newswire, TheSoul × Kidoodle](https://prnewswire.com:443/news-releases/thesoul-publishings-family-friendly-content-comes-to-global-safe-streaming-platform-301668621.html); [Wikipedia, Kidoodle.TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidoodle.TV)
- **pocket.watch**, a kids creator studio (Ryan's World and others), has the most-distributed US kids and family FAST channels via a Fubo deal. — [Toybook](https://toybook.com/pocket-watch-fubo-fast-channels-partnership)
- Common Sense Networks launched three Sensical FAST channels with suppliers including Moonbug. Moonbug launched COPPA-compliant free channels with Future Today in 2021. — [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=203129); [Animation Magazine, 2021](https://animationmagazine.net/2021/01/moonbug-teams-with-future-today-to-launch-5-new-kids-channels)
- One study counted **82 distinct kids FAST channels across 21 services**. — [The Streamable](https://thestreamable.com/news/analysis-kids-programming-boosts-fast-channel-identities)

### Inferences
- **For YouTube Kids:** follow the quality principles (gentle humour, no frightening slapstick, no clickbait), keep the channel MFK, and build playlists. Inclusion is algorithmic and curated, not applied for.
- **FAST and licensing path:** keep **horizontal 16:9 master renders** or recompositions and longer compilations of 3–11 min. FAST and AVOD buyers program in TV formats, not 9:16. A library of 100+ shorts compiled into 7–11 min episodes is the typical unit to pitch to Kidoodle.TV, Sensical, or aggregators like pocket.watch, Future Today and Moonbug-style networks.
- If the 2027 20M-Shorts-views rule is real, a Shorts-only kids channel faces a harder entry to YPP. That strengthens the case for adding long-form compilations, which also earn more per view.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 source on Kidoodle.TV or Sensical submission processes for independent creators.
- YouTube Kids' current eligibility rules for **Shorts** specifically were not found.
- Licensing deal economics (flat fees or revenue share) for small IP were not found.

## 6. Cross-posting best practices (same file vs. tailored, order, watermarks, safe zones)

### Takeaway
The only hard, sourced rule is Meta's originality system: reposts are demoted, and since 2026 mostly-unoriginal accounts lose recommendation reach. The rest is inference from platform mechanics. Export one **clean master with no watermark**, upload it **natively** to each platform from the brand's own account, and post YouTube first as the canonical original. Keep on-screen text minimal and inside the central safe area. Tailor only length (for TikTok monetization) and the first frame or caption.

### Cited Findings
- Identical reposts are replaced by the original in Instagram recommendations. Significant edits are exempt. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- Account-level demotion of mostly-unoriginal accounts applies from April 2026. — [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/)
- TikTok CRP requires original videos of 1+ min and excludes duets and stitches. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-creativity-program/)

### Inferences
- **Same file vs. tailored:**
  - YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and Facebook Reels get the same 1080×1920 master.
  - TikTok gets the same master for reach, plus weekly >60 s compilations for CRP eligibility if the creator's country qualifies.
  - Long-form YouTube (MFK) and FAST get horizontal compilations.
- **Posting order:**
  1. YouTube first, establishing the earliest timestamp as the original.
  2. Then TikTok, Instagram and Facebook within 24 h from the same brand handle.
  3. Use Meta's Collab feature or Facebook/Instagram cross-posting from the same Meta account, which keeps a single original.
- **Watermarks:** never re-upload a file downloaded from TikTok or CapCut. Export from the editor without logos. The originality systems match visual fingerprints, and visible third-party logos signal "repost".
- **On-screen text and safe zones:** keep the characters and any text in roughly the central 1080×1420 area. Avoid about the top 250 px and bottom 400–450 px, where UI overlays sit, and the right roughly 120 px, where action buttons sit. These pixel values are common industry guidance and were not sourced here. A non-verbal series should avoid language-specific on-screen text altogether, which preserves universality. If a title card is used, a short logo card "Mochi & Pip" works in every market.
- **Captions:**
  - Write per-platform captions in English plus the local language of the top market.
  - Hashtags on TikTok and Instagram should frame the content as family or cat comedy, not "for kids".
  - On YouTube, keep MFK and use translated titles.

### Gaps
- No official 2025–2026 safe-zone pixel specs were retrieved for each platform.
- No data on whether posting order affects originality attribution across platforms. Meta's system is intra-Meta, as far as the sources show.

## 7. Music and sound per platform; building recognizability with original music

### Takeaway
Original synthesized music is the right choice for MFK YouTube: no licensing risk, and no Content ID conflicts with others. For TikTok, original audio becomes a reusable "sound" automatically when uploaded. The theme can also be distributed formally through **SoundOn**, TikTok's own distributor launched in 2022, which places songs in TikTok's library and on streaming services. I found no source on uploading a theme to the YouTube Audio Library. Registering own music in Content ID through a distributor is possible in principle, but it risks claims against the channel's own cross-posts and reposts.

### Cited Findings
- **SoundOn** is TikTok's music distribution and marketing platform, launched in March 2022. It places songs on TikTok and other streaming services, and offers audience insights and song-tab placement. Terms at launch were free distribution, 100% royalties in year 1 and 90% after. These 2022 terms need re-checking. — [TechCrunch, 9 Mar 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/03/09/tiktok-launches-a-music-distribution-platform-soundon/); [The Line of Best Fit](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/news/latest-news/tiktok-launches-its-own-music-marketing-and-distribution-platform-soundon); [Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2022/mar/9/tiktok-launches-its-own-music-distribution-platfor/); [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/read/soundon-music-distributing-marketing-service-tech)

### Inferences
- **Recognisability strategy:**
  1. Use one short **signature sting** of 2–3 s, the same in every episode.
  2. Name the TikTok original sound "Mochi & Pip Theme", so every use links back.
  3. Optionally distribute the theme via SoundOn or another distributor to Spotify, YouTube Music and similar services.
- **Content ID caution:** if a distributor registers the theme in Content ID, the creator's own uploads and any licensed partner uploads can receive claims. If distribution happens, the channel should be whitelisted or allowlisted, or Content ID registration should be opted out of for the theme.
- **Trending sounds:** on TikTok and Reels, trending audio helps discovery, but it would replace the original score and the brand recognisability. On YouTube MFK, third-party music risks claims. A reasonable compromise is the original score for all episodes, with occasional trending-sound teasers on TikTok and Instagram only.
- **YouTube Audio Library:** it is a curated library, and I found no evidence of an open submission path for creators. Treat this as not viable.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on YouTube Audio Library submissions, YouTube Content ID eligibility for small creators' original music, or current SoundOn terms in 2025–2026.

## Legal and regulatory constraints affecting distribution (brief)

### Takeaway
COPPA in the US is why MFK content has no personalized ads and why its RPM is low. TikTok and Meta are 13+ services. India's TikTok ban removes the largest volume market from TikTok. Other regimes (GDPR-K, India's DPDP Act) mainly constrain platforms' data handling for minors. Indirectly, they push platforms to restrict kids-targeted distribution and ads.

### Cited Findings
- MFK content cannot receive personalized ads under COPPA, which lowers CPMs. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-cpm/)
- TikTok requires users to be 13+ in most of the world, and runs a curated under-13 mode only in the US. — [TikTok Transparency](https://www.tiktok.com/transparency/en/protecting-teens/)
- The India TikTok ban (Section 69A, June 2020) was reaffirmed in Aug 2025. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/no-comeback-for-tiktok-indian-govt-refutes-reports-of-ban-being-lifted-on-chinese-apps-490769-2025-08-23)

### Inferences
- The creator's best defence is to keep YouTube MFK honest. Mislabeling kids content as not-MFK risks FTC/COPPA exposure and YouTube penalties. On the 13+ platforms, the content should be framed as family or all-ages comedy and should never solicit children's data or engagement.

### Gaps
- India's DPDP Act (2023) and its 2025 rules on verifiable parental consent, and GDPR Article 8 age thresholds, were not researched in this pass. Their direct effect on a creator posting cartoons is likely minimal, since they bind platforms and data fiduciaries, but this was not verified.

# YouTube "Made for Kids", content-quality/AI/mass-production policies and monetization economics for "Mochi & Pip" (state as of Nov 2026)

> Research context: a Shorts-only, dialogue-free cat-and-mouse cartoon, 23–45 s, 1080x1920, fully code-generated (procedural 2D/3D, synthesized music, AI coding assistant), set as Made for Kids (MFK), 1 upload/day, same characters and recurring formula.
>
> Method note: in this session the network sandbox blocked direct fetches of support.google.com, blog.youtube, techcrunch.com, tubefilter.com and dexerto.com. Findings below come from search-engine extracts of those pages (official Help Center text, YouTube Blog, FTC pages, trade press) plus one direct fetch of youtube.com/howyoutubeworks. Where wording is paraphrased from search extracts rather than read in full, it is flagged. Official policy vs. third-party/creator anecdote is labelled throughout: **[OFFICIAL]**, **[PRESS]**, **[VENDOR/ANECDOTE]**.

## 1. What MFK disables, and how MFK status affects Shorts recommendation and growth

### Takeaway
MFK switches off every viewer-data-dependent feature: personalized ads, comments, the notification bell, save to playlist, cards/end screens, memberships, posts, Super Chat/Stickers, and autoplay on Home. Growth relies almost entirely on algorithmic recommendation next to other kids' content. YouTube publishes no rule that keeps MFK Shorts out of the regular Shorts feed. It does say MFK videos are "more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos", so the effective audience is young children watching on shared/family devices and YouTube Kids, not adults.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** For MFK videos, these features are unavailable: autoplay on home, cards or end screens, Super Chat or Super Stickers, and save to playlist. At channel level (when the channel is set MFK), channel memberships, notifications, posts (Community) and comments are unavailable. YouTube gives COPPA compliance as the reason: it limits data collection on MFK content — [YouTube Help "Watching made for kids content" (9632097)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9632097?hl=en); [YouTube Help "Set your channel or video's audience" (9527654)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** "YouTube does not serve personalised ads on 'Made for Kids' content, and some features are not available on these videos, like comments and notifications" — [YouTube Help "Best practices for kids & family content" (10774223)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)
- **[OFFICIAL]** A YouTube creator page states that videos set as MFK are "more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos" (search extract) — [YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube's kids & family **quality principles** "inform the recommendation system so that higher-quality videos are surfaced" — [How YouTube Works: Kids and teens](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/kids-and-teens/) (fetched directly). YouTube also says "high-quality content will be recommended more by the YouTube algorithm going forward, in addition to being included in the YouTube Kids app", and that applying the principles to YouTube Kids recommendations raised viewership of high-quality content by "over 45%" in the app (YouTube's own figure) — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223); [YouTube Blog, "Enabling a high quality kids experience"](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/enabling-high-quality-youtube-kids-experience/)
- **[PRESS]** Tubefilter covered the January 2020 rollout of disabled features on kid-oriented content — [Tubefilter, 2020-01-07](https://www.tubefilter.com/2020/01/07/youtube-disables-more-product-features-kid-oriented-content/amp/)
- **[PRESS – evidence that MFK Shorts reach toddlers at scale]** The New York Times investigation (late Feb 2026) found that after a single CoComelon video, more than 40% of the recommended **Shorts** in a 15-minute session contained synthetic/AI visuals. The Times reviewed more than 1,000 videos recommended to young children. So kids' Shorts are served heavily through the Shorts feed to child viewers — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/); [dnyuz mirror of NYT, 2026-02-26](https://dnyuz.com/2026/02/26/how-a-i-generated-videos-are-distorting-your-childs-youtube-feed/)
- **[VENDOR/ANECDOTE – MFK Shorts growth cases]** AIR Media-Tech (a network that sells services) reports these cases: kids channels "PlayToys & Play Dolls" were missing from YouTube Kids until a review by YouTube's kids team, after which their Shorts reached 887K views ([AIR](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/1m-views-with-shorts-getting-on-youtube-kids)); a kids channel whose Shorts launch brought +52% total channel views and +33% revenue in two weeks ([AIR](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/336-revenue-with-less-long-form-content-kids-channel-case-study)); "WOA Song Spanish" grew views +5,324% after a "kids content quality reset" ([AIR](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/5324-views-and-3453-revenue-woa-song-spanish-case)). These are unaudited marketing figures.
- **[PRESS]** Kids/animation channels regularly appear in Tubefilter's weekly "Top 50 most viewed channels" charts in 2025, which shows that MFK/kids content still reaches very large view volumes — [Tubefilter Top 50, week of 2025-06-16](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/06/16/top-50-most-viewed-youtube-channels-week-of-06-15-2025/)
- **[VENDOR/ANECDOTE]** A Dec 2025 forum post claims older Shorts views "basically die after ~30 days". This is anecdotal and not MFK-specific — [TunePocket forum](https://www.tunepocket.com/community/topic/important-youtube-shorts-algorithm-update)

### Inferences
- Because the subscribe bell, comments and playlists are off, MFK Shorts cannot build the usual retention loop through notifications and community. Daily views depend on feed placement and on rewatch/loop rate among children. Subscriber counts tend to grow slowly compared with views.
- With "more likely to be recommended alongside other kids' videos", an MFK cat-and-mouse Short competes in the same pool as CoComelon-adjacent and AI kids Shorts. That pool is now under the heaviest scrutiny (see §5).
- Adults will see MFK Shorts mainly on shared/family devices or after watching kids content. There is no evidence of meaningful reach to adult Shorts audiences, and no official statement confirms or denies it.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube text was found on whether MFK Shorts are excluded from, or down-weighted in, the general (adult) Shorts feed. This could not be verified because the Help Center was not directly fetchable.
- Background knowledge, **not verified in this session**: MFK also disables the mini-player, background play, info cards, Stories (now retired), the merch shelf/product shelf, YouTube Shopping, Super Thanks, the "Remix"/Shorts reuse features, and live chat. These should be checked on Help article 9528076 ("Determining if your content is made for kids") and 9632097.
- No independent (non-vendor) dataset was found on MFK Shorts growth rates.

## 2. Should Mochi & Pip be marked MFK? FTC/COPPA criteria, mislabeling consequences, "general audience" option

### Takeaway
A dialogue-free animated cat-and-mouse cartoon with simple slapstick, bright colors and short runtime matches several FTC/YouTube "child-directed" factors: animated characters, kid-appealing subject matter, visual style and music. MFK is the defensible default. Labelling it "general audience" to unlock comments, the bell and personalized ads is legitimate only if the content is genuinely made for a broader audience (e.g., humor, pacing and themes aimed at teens/adults, like classic theatrical cartoons that targeted general audiences). YouTube can override the setting, and the FTC treats channel owners as operators liable for COPPA civil penalties.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL – FTC]** The FTC treats a channel owner on a third-party platform as if it ran its own website or app for COPPA purposes. Child-directed status is judged on several factors: subject matter, visual content, use of **animated characters**, music, age of models, and others. Animation alone does not automatically trigger coverage, and a channel is not child-directed just because some children watch it — [FTC (search extract of FTC-hosted page)](https://www.ftc.gov/comment/5303); [FTC consumer blog "YouTube pays big for tracking kids" (2019)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/blog/2019/09/youtube-pays-big-tracking-kids)
- **[OFFICIAL – FTC]** The Sept 2019 settlement cost Google/YouTube $170M ($136M to the FTC + $34M to New York), the largest COPPA penalty since 1998. It required YouTube to build a system for channel owners to designate child-directed content, and YouTube said it would use machine learning to identify child-directed videos. The FTC said it would "sweep" YouTube to check designations — [FTC press release, 2019-09-04](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/09/google-youtube-will-pay-record-170-million-alleged-violations-childrens-privacy-law); [TechCrunch 2019: creators may be held liable](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/04/youtube-creators-may-also-be-held-liable-for-coppa-violations-following-ftc-settlement); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/ftc-touts-historic-youtube-settlement-as-game-changer-for-coppa-enforcement)
- **[OFFICIAL – FTC, dated]** An FTC-hosted page cited a per-violation civil penalty maximum of **$42,530** (2019-era figure). The amount is inflation-adjusted annually, so the current figure is higher — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/comment/5298)
- **[PRESS]** In YouTube's list of made-for-kids signals, toys, animated characters and child actors make kids' animation higher-risk for MFK classification — [channelterminated.com (secondary)](https://channelterminated.com/channel-types/ai-slop/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** In YouTube's settings, creators must set the audience at channel or video level. YouTube may change the designation if it detects error or abuse (search extract) — [YouTube Help 9527654](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en); [Studio Content Manager 9610285](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9610285)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Adult-targeted cartoons can stay up (age-restricted). Family-targeted cartoons with mature themes are removed (see §6). This shows YouTube distinguishes "family/kids" from "general/adult" animation by targeting signals in the title, description, tags and style — [YouTube Child safety policy (2801999)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999)

### Inferences
- Mochi & Pip as described (no dialogue, cute cat and mouse, simple gags, synthesized cheerful music, 23–45 s) is primarily appealing to preschool/early-school children. Marking it "not MFK" mainly to regain features would be the classic mislabeling risk. YouTube's classifier would likely flip it to MFK anyway, and the creator would carry COPPA exposure.
- A credible "general audience" route requires changing the product, not just the setting. Examples: theatrical-cartoon-style humor and timing for all ages, metadata and thumbnails not aimed at kids (no "for kids/toddlers/nursery" keywords), no educational-for-toddlers framing, and targeting teens/adults through references, pacing and tone. Even then, cartoon cat-and-mouse is a borderline case: classic Tom & Jerry-style cartoons were made for general theatrical audiences but are widely consumed by children.
- Recommended: keep MFK if the target is children. A "mixed audience" approach is possible, setting individual videos per-video, but the content must actually differ.

### Gaps
- The 2025 amended COPPA Rule was not retrieved in this session. Background knowledge, **unverified here**: it was published April 2025, effective June 23, 2025, with a compliance deadline around April 22, 2026, and the 2025 inflation-adjusted penalty is about $53,088 per violation. Both figures need verification on ftc.gov.
- No public case was found of the FTC fining an individual YouTube animation creator. The 2019 statements warn of liability, but no enforcement examples were located.

## 3. YouTube Kids app: eligibility, how content gets in, quality principles

### Takeaway
There is no application process. YouTube Kids draws from MFK content on main YouTube, filtered by automated systems plus human review, with age tiers (Preschool ≤4, Younger 5–8, Older 9–12). The "quality principles for kids and family content" (in force since Nov 2021) govern YouTube Kids inclusion, recommendation boosts and monetization. Low-quality MFK content gets limited or no ads, and a channel focused on it can be suspended from YPP. YPP suspension also blocks a channel from YouTube Kids.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Kids is "an app built from the ground up to be a simpler experience for kids 12 and under", with four viewing experiences. Parents can approve content themselves or choose age-based categories — [How YouTube Works: Kids and teens](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/kids-and-teens/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Content limits by YouTube Kids tier: the "Younger" setting allows non-graphic, slapstick cartoon violence. "Preschool" is limited to non-violent, non-scary scripted/animated videos (search extract) — [YouTube Help "Content policies for YouTube Kids" (10938174)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **Low-quality** examples in the quality principles: heavily commercial or promotional content (toy/brand-centric, unboxing that encourages excessive buying); content that encourages negative behaviors or attitudes; **deceptively educational** content; content that **hinders comprehension**; sensational or misleading content. The list is not exhaustive — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223); [PDF "Best practices for kids and family content"](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/best_practices_for_kids_and_family_content_en.pdf)
- **[OFFICIAL]** **High-quality** principles (per YouTube's best-practices page and PDF) cover content that is age-appropriate, enriching, engaging and inspiring. They include promoting healthy habits/being a good person, learning and inspiring curiosity, creativity/play/imagination, and real-world connection — [YouTube quality principles PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/hytw_yt_quality_content_principles.pdf); [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Monetization consequences: an individual video that violates the principles may see **limited or no ads**. A channel with a strong focus on low-quality MFK content **may be suspended from YPP**. Channels that mainly target young audiences must deliver high-quality content and comply with kids-specific monetization policies — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223); [TechCrunch, 2021-10-25 "YouTube warns creators it will start to demonetize low-quality kids videos next month"](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/25/youtube-warns-creators-it-will-start-to-demonetize-low-quality-kids-videos-next-month)
- **[PRESS]** After the Feb 2026 NYT investigation, YouTube suspended five named channels from YPP. YPP suspension means no ad revenue **and** being blocked from appearing on YouTube Kids. YouTube said it is developing dedicated AI labels for YouTube Kids, with no timeline — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/); [Fortune, 2026-04-01](https://www.fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google)
- **[VENDOR/ANECDOTE]** An agency reports a kids channel became searchable in YouTube Kids only after review by YouTube's kids team for safety/age-appropriateness — [AIR](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/1m-views-with-shorts-getting-on-youtube-kids)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube is extending quality principles to teens (separate "Best practices for teen content" page) — [YouTube Help 16839968](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16839968)

### Inferences
- A wordless slapstick cat-and-mouse cartoon fits "creativity, play, imagination" and is not promotional. Its main quality risks are (a) "hindering comprehension" through hyper-fast, chaotic, sensory-overload editing, (b) "sensational" thumbnails/titles, and (c) gags that model negative behavior such as cruelty or bullying without consequence. Episodes with a clear mini-story arc, a resolution, a positive social beat (cooperation, sharing, problem-solving) and calm pacing align with the "high-quality" side.
- Gentle slapstick (falls, bonks, no injury) fits YouTube Kids "Younger" but may exclude the "Preschool" tier.

### Gaps
- No public, current (2025–2026) official list of the exact high-quality examples could be read in full. Only search extracts were available.
- No data on how long YouTube Kids inclusion takes or whether Shorts are surfaced in YouTube Kids as Shorts (one non-official wiki says they appear as regular videos).

## 4. YPP thresholds, Shorts revenue sharing, MFK monetization, kids RPM

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, there are two entry tiers. The lower tier (500 subs + 3M Shorts views/90 days, plus 3 uploads) unlocks fan funding/Shopping, but most of those features are disabled on MFK anyway. The full tier (1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views/90 days) unlocks ad revenue. On **Feb 1, 2027** the full tier rises to **20M Shorts views/90 days** for new applicants, and *all* YPP creators will need **≥10M qualified Shorts views per rolling 90 days** to earn Shorts ad revenue. Shorts creators keep 45% of their allocated share of a monthly Creator Pool, after a music-licensing carve-out when licensed music is used. MFK only gets contextual (non-personalized) ads, and kids RPMs are among the lowest on the platform, roughly $0.02–$0.10 per 1,000 Shorts views.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Full YPP eligibility: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public watch hours (12 months) **or** 1,000 subscribers + **10 million valid public Shorts views in the last 90 days**. Excluded from the Shorts count: private, unlisted and deleted Shorts, ad campaigns, and image posts in the Shorts feed — [YouTube Help "YPP overview & eligibility" (72851)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?rd=1)
- **[PRESS / OFFICIAL blog]** Lower tier (fan funding + Shopping): 500 subscribers + 3M valid public Shorts views in 90 days (or 3,000 watch hours), plus at least 3 public uploads in the last 90 days. This tier is unchanged in the 2026 announcement — [YouTube Blog "New opportunities to earn…"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-opportunities-to-earn-and-changes-to-the-youtube-partner-program/); [Tubefilter, 2026-08-10](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)
- **[PRESS / OFFICIAL blog]** Announced **Aug 10, 2026**: from **Feb 1, 2027**, full-monetization requirements rise from 4,000 to **8,000** watch hours or from 10M to **20M** qualified Shorts views (90 days), with 1,000 subscribers unchanged. Also from Feb 1, 2027, creators will need **at least 10M qualified Shorts views in a rolling 90-day window to receive ad and Premium revenue from Shorts**, even if already in YPP. Existing members are grandfathered on entry thresholds — [YouTube Blog "qualified watch hours / Shorts views"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-monetization-qualified-watch-hours-shorts-views/); [NewscastStudio, 2026-08-10](https://www.newscaststudio.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-monetization-changes-2027/); [exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/industry-briefing-news/youtube-sets-10-million-shorts-views-for-monetisation-from-2027-157245.html); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Shorts revenue model: each month, revenue from ads shown between videos in the Shorts Feed is pooled. The pool rewards Shorts creators and covers **music licensing** costs. If a Short uses music, the revenue tied to its views is split between the **Creator Pool** and music partners based on the number of tracks. Monetizing creators keep **45%** of their allocated revenue whether or not music was used. Only views of content following the **advertiser-friendly guidelines** count — [YouTube Help "YouTube Shorts monetization policies" (12504220)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en); [YouTube Blog "Supporting the next wave of creative entrepreneurs" (Sept 2022)](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/supporting-the-next-wave-of-creative-entrepreneurs/); [TechCrunch 2023-01-09 (start Feb 1, 2023)](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/09/youtube-new-partner-program-terms-shorts-revenue-sharing-february-1/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** New: from **Feb 1, 2027**, Shorts ads targeted at small groups of ≤5 channels become eligible for direct revenue, with the creator keeping 45% of net revenue from that ad. This is forward-dated — [YouTube Help 12504220](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** MFK content gets **no personalized ads** and only contextual ads — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)
- **[VENDOR]** Kids & Teens long-form median RPM is **$0.33**, attributed to the MFK ban on personalized ads — [AIR Media-Tech "Which niche makes the most money in 2026"](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)
- **[VENDOR]** Shorts RPM ranges from **$0.02 to $1.48** depending on niche, about 3–14% of long-form RPM. Sample: 274 channels — [AIR Media-Tech Shorts RPM study](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- **[VENDOR]** Other kids RPM estimates: $1–3 per 1,000 views ([gyre.pro](https://gyre.pro/blog/how-to-monetize-a-youtube-kids-channel)) and $0.50–$2 ([milx](https://milx.app/en/guidelines/guide-to-monetize-videos-in-youtube-kids-niche)). Neither is based on disclosed data. A 300-channel dataset (May 2025–May 2026) shows general RPM peaking at **$2.35 in November** and falling to **$1.56 in January**, and kids RPM up about 22% in Q4 — [milx CPM/RPM 2026](https://milx.app/en/trends/youtube-cpm-rpm-rates-2026-average-niches-countries-more)
- **[VENDOR, small sample]** In one kids analysis, 45.7% of Shorts views carried an ad vs 12.1% of long-form views (3 channels per format). It claims Shorts can become the main revenue driver for large kids channels — [pravinzende.co.in (2026-01)](https://www.pravinzende.co.in/2026/01/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-for-kid-creators-2026.html)

### Inferences
- Order-of-magnitude example, not a forecast: at a kids Shorts RPM of about $0.03–$0.08, reaching 10M Shorts views per 90 days yields roughly $100–$270/month. 100M views/month might yield about $3,000–$8,000/month. Shorts-only kids monetization only pays at very large scale. From Feb 2027, a channel that drops below 10M Shorts views per 90 days earns $0 from Shorts ads even if already in YPP.
- Synthesized original music is an advantage: the creator keeps the full allocation instead of splitting with music partners.
- Since MFK disables memberships, Super Thanks, merch shelf and Shopping, the 500-sub lower tier is of little value to an MFK channel. Realistic revenue levers are ads, plus long-form compilations (which have higher RPM than Shorts and are commonly used by kids channels), plus off-platform licensing or merch.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube RPM figures for MFK exist. All numbers come from vendors/agencies with undisclosed methods.
- How the creator share of the pool is computed (proportional to the creator's share of total eligible Shorts views, from background knowledge) was not re-verified here.
- No data on whether MFK Shorts views count toward the pool identically to non-MFK views. They should, but this was not confirmed.

## 5. Inauthentic / reused / mass-produced / AI content, AI disclosure, and the 2025–2026 kids-AI crackdowns

### Takeaway
The policy that matters is the YPP **"inauthentic content"** policy, renamed from "repetitious content" on **July 15, 2025** and expanded on about **July 13–16, 2026** into explicit sub-categories: generic, repetitive or template-based content; "unsatisfying/off-putting or distressing" content; and AI personas on sensitive topics. It targets *mass-produced, template-based, low-variation* output whatever the tool. Code-generated animation is not banned, and "clearly unrealistic/animated" content does **not** require the altered/synthetic disclosure. However, a one-per-day, same-formula, auto-generated kids series is the exact profile hit in 2026: NYT-driven YPP suspensions in Feb 2026, and false positives on human animators in Feb–May 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL via press]** On **July 15, 2025**, YouTube updated YPP guidelines "to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content". "Repetitious content" was renamed **"inauthentic content"**, meaning mass-produced or repetitive content often perceived as spam. YouTube called it a "minor update"; creator liaison Rene Ritchie said such content was already ineligible. YouTube's examples: narrated stories with only superficial differences, and slideshows with identical narration. Consequence: suspension or removal from YPP — [TechCrunch, 2025-07-09](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-targets-mass-produced-content-in-monetization-update/550337/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [Plagiarism Today 2025-07-08](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)
- **[OFFICIAL via press]** **July 2026 clarification**: creators can't monetize "generic or repetitive content", content that is "unsatisfying or off-putting", or channels with fake AI "experts". The updated guidelines cover generic, repetitive or **template-based** content; off-putting or distressing content; and AI personas on sensitive topics (health, finance, legal, medical). AI personas in general may be allowed — [Tubefilter, 2026-07-13](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/); [TechCrunch, 2026-07-20](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-rules-inauthentic-content-amid-ai-generated-video-concerns-12183810)
- **[OFFICIAL – disclosure]** The "altered or synthetic content" disclosure (launched March 2024) is required for *realistic* content. Creators "don't have to disclose content that is clearly unrealistic, animated, includes special effects, or has used generative AI for production assistance". For unrealistic/animated content, a label (if applied) appears in the expanded description, not on the player. YouTube may auto-label content its systems detect as AI-generated. A disclosure label alone does not change recommendations or monetization eligibility — [YouTube Help "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content" (14328491)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491); [YouTube Blog, Mar 2024](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/disclosing-ai-generated-content); [9to5Google 2024-03-18](https://9to5google.com/2024/03/18/youtube-altered-content-disclosure/)
- **[PRESS]** In the NYT coverage, YouTube's realistic-AI disclosure rule "doesn't apply to animated videos for children". Since then, 200+ organizations and experts have demanded that YouTube label all AI content, ban it from YouTube Kids, and prohibit AI-generated MFK content on main YouTube. YouTube said it is developing AI labels for YouTube Kids — [Fortune, 2026-04-01](https://www.fortune.com/2026/04/01/ai-slop-200-organizations-letter-youtube-google); [Yahoo/Fast Company syndication](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/ai-slop-flooding-youtube-kids-201112658.html)
- **[PRESS] Wave 1 – Feb 2026, NYT investigation**: YouTube suspended the five channels the Times named from YPP (no ads, removed from YouTube Kids) and removed a clip under child-safety rules. Problems cited: characters with warped faces or extra limbs, garbled text, misinformation, "deceptively educational" content. YouTube said the 15 channels in the article were not on YouTube Kids, which other reports dispute — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/investigation-finds-youtube-is-serving-mindless-ai-slop-to-toddlers-and-preschoolers/); [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/youtube-strikes-down-channels-after-investigation-into-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids-3326231/); [veefly blog](https://blog.veefly.com/latest-youtube-updates/youtube-stops-bizarre-ai-content-for-kids/)

### Inferences
- **Disclosure:** procedurally/code-generated cartoon animation is "clearly unrealistic/animated", so the altered/synthetic disclosure is not required under current text. Using an AI coding assistant is "production assistance". Ticking the box anyway is low-cost (description-only label, no stated reach effect) and may matter if YouTube Kids AI labels launch. If generative-AI imagery or voices were ever added, re-check the rule.
- **Main risk is YPP review, not removal.** YPP reviewers judge the channel as a whole. A daily feed of same-length, same-setup, same-music Shorts with interchangeable gags reads as "template-based with little variation", especially in the kids/AI niche that is now under spotlight and NGO pressure.
- **Practices that keep a templated series on the safe side** (derived from policy language and the cases above):
  1. Each episode has a distinct, nameable story: setup → escalation → payoff, with a unique gag, location, prop or conflict. Avoid the same plot with swapped colors/objects (the policy's "superficial differences" example).
  2. Vary episode length, camera, staging, settings, seasons, supporting characters and musical themes. Compose distinct cues per episode instead of one loop.
  3. Visible human authorship: a written premise/logline per episode, a series bible, storyboards/animatics, and titles/descriptions that describe the specific story. Keep production logs (scripts, commits, storyboards) as appeal evidence.
  4. Avoid AI-slop tells: no glitches, morphing anatomy, garbled on-screen text, nonsensical "educational" claims.
  5. Quality over cadence. One upload per day is not prohibited, but a lower cadence with more variety is safer in YPP review than pure volume.
  6. Add long-form compilations with value added (themed collections plus new bridging content), not just raw re-uploads, which could fall under "reused content".
- "Reused content" (a separate YPP policy) mainly concerns other people's content. It applies here only if compilations re-upload Shorts without meaningful change.

## 6. Kids-content safety rules: mature themes, slapstick violence, dangerous acts

### Takeaway
YouTube's **Child safety policy** removes (not just demonetizes) family-targeted content with violence, sex, death, drugs or other mature themes. Cartoons and familiar characters used to lure kids into such content were the core of "Elsagate" (2017) and of the August 2019 policy update. Non-graphic slapstick is allowed: YouTube Kids "Younger" explicitly allows "non-graphic, slapstick cartoon violence". But graphic injury, cruelty, gore, scary transformations, or imitable dangerous acts in a kids cartoon cross the line.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** "Content that targets young minors and families but contains sexual themes, violence, obscene, or other mature themes not suitable for young audiences, is not allowed on YouTube." Named examples include "family friendly cartoons that target young minors and contain adult or age-inappropriate themes such as violence, sex, death, drugs" and using "cartoons, puppets or family entertainment characters" to appeal to children when the content features adult themes — [YouTube Child safety policy (2801999)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999); [How YouTube Works: Kids and teens](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/kids-and-teens/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Educational/documentary/scientific/artistic exceptions are "not a pass to target young minors and families with mature themes intended to shock young audiences" — [YouTube Help 2801999](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999)
- **[PRESS]** August 2019 update: YouTube began removing content with violent or mature themes when targeted at kids through title, description or tags, in response to 2017's "Elsagate" — [Tubefilter, 2019-08-23](https://tubefilter.com/2019/08/23/youtube-updates-child-safety-policies-to-remove-adult-themed-videos-aimed-at-kids/); [The Verge via Slashdot](https://news.slashdot.org/story/19/08/23/2218212/youtube-says-it-will-now-remove-violent-and-mature-videos-pretending-to-be-kid-friendly); [YouTube Blog "5 ways we're toughening our approach" (2017)](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/5-ways-were-toughening-our-approach-to?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Kids tiers: "Younger" allows non-graphic slapstick cartoon violence. "Preschool" is limited to non-violent, non-scary animated content — [YouTube Help 10938174](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL – Google publisher policy]** Google ad policies separately restrict "Adult themes in family content" for ad serving — [Google Publisher Policies 10437484](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/10437484)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Quality principles flag content that "encourages negative behaviors or attitudes" (e.g., meanness or dangerous behavior) as low quality, which limits ads — [YouTube Help 10774223](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223)

### Inferences
- For Mochi & Pip: keep cat-vs-mouse conflict cartoony and reversible, with no blood, no visible pain or crying-in-agony, no weapons aimed at characters, no death jokes, and no "trap" gags that kids could imitate (electrical outlets, knives, fire, medicine, plastic bags, climbing furniture). Prefer rivalry that turns into cooperation. This also scores better on the quality principles and fits YouTube Kids "Preschool/Younger".
- The classic "Tom & Jerry" register (hammers, explosions, flattening) is exactly where the Younger-tier allowance ends and the quality principle against "negative behaviors" begins. Tone it down.

## 7. Copyright/trademark: similarity to Tom & Jerry and other cartoons

### Takeaway
The premise ("cat chases/rivals a mouse", wordless slapstick) is an unprotectable idea or *scènes à faire*. What is protected is specific expression: character designs, names, specific gags and sequences, music, logos. Warner Bros. Discovery also holds trademarks in "Tom and Jerry" and the character images. Mochi & Pip is safe as long as designs, names, color schemes, title cards and music are clearly original, and no thumbnails/titles/tags trade on "Tom and Jerry".

### Cited Findings
- **[LEGAL – general doctrine]** Copyright protects a particular story, characters or artwork, "but generally not … the idea or genre of the story". Under *scènes à faire*, expression that necessarily flows from a commonplace idea is not protected — [Wikipedia: Idea–expression distinction](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Idea-expression_divide)
- **[LEGAL – commentary]** An anthropomorphic, flying, caped mouse hero is an unprotectable idea, while the specific depiction of Mighty Mouse is protectable. A basic character concept is an idea, not expression — [UNH IP Mall, Hilton (IDEA journal)](https://ipmall.law.unh.edu/sites/default/files/hosted_resources/IDEA/p269.Hilton.pdf)
- **[LEGAL – recent]** A recent federal ruling held that "style, no matter how creative, is an idea" and not protectable by copyright — [Stanford Law, "How generative AI disrupts traditional copyright law"](https://law.stanford.edu/press/how-generative-ai-disrupts-traditional-copyright-law)

### Inferences
- What to avoid: a grey-blue cat plus a brown mouse with Tom & Jerry silhouettes/proportions; names, logos or title-card typography evocative of MGM/Hanna-Barbera; recreating specific famous gags shot by shot; Tom & Jerry-style orchestral music or any sampled score; using "Tom and Jerry" in titles, tags or descriptions to capture search (trademark and YouTube "misleading metadata" risk). An orange cat named Mochi and a mouse named Pip, with original designs and synthesized music, sits on the "idea" side.
- On YouTube the practical enforcement path is Content ID/copyright takedowns (3 strikes = termination) and trademark complaints. Distinct designs and an original soundtrack minimise both.
- Also check that "Mochi & Pip" is not an existing registered children's-media mark (USPTO/EUIPO search) before investing in merch.

- No clearance search was performed on the "Mochi & Pip" name.
